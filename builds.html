Plate I — BionicsIn development

Flapping-wing ornithopter

A drone that flies like an insect: two pairs of transparent membrane wings, driven by a printed gear train instead of propellers.

The wings are thin film stretched over a printed vein frame — light enough to flap, stiff enough to push air. The whole aircraft is built around one rule: every gram counts.

Data sheet
BrainXIAO ESP32-S3 Sense + camera
Drive6 mm coreless motor
Reduction1 : 25.4, printed involute gears
Structure1 mm carbon rods
ControlWiFi, live camera view
Target mass≈ 22 g
BiomimicryESP323D print
Plate II — MechanismPrototype

Printed micro gearbox

A transparent gear train that turns a tiny fast motor into a slow, strong flapping stroke.

Gears are generated parametrically with true involute profiles and printed in clear resin-look filament, then pinned and checked stage by stage.

Plate III — Fixed wingFlying

FPV aircraft

A foam-board aircraft with a first-person video link — flown through the camera.

Ailerons steer it: deflect one down and the other up, and the pressure difference between the two wings rolls the aircraft.

Plate IV — TaillessFlying

Flying wing

No tail at all — the wing is the aircraft.

Two control surfaces do the work of three: moving together they pitch the nose, moving opposite they roll. Winglets keep it tracking straight.

Plate V — GroundBuilt

Micro RC car

An ultralight radio-controlled car cut from foam sheet — the same weight-first thinking, on the ground.

A coreless motor drives the wheel directly, and thin steel pushrods carry the steering from the onboard electronics.
